### New Forms of Products

If you consider a **Product - as something which solves a relevant problem**, you won’t have any issues moving through different product shapes.

**API AS A PRODUCT**

The product is a capability that other people's code uses, so your user is a developer. Success means a developer can get value fast and on their own.

---

**Stripe**

Stripe sells payments as a few lines of code. Its developer docs and quick setup are the product experience, and that's why so many companies built on it and never left.

**DATA - AS - A - SERVICE**

The product is the data itself: clean, trusted and kept up to date, delivered on demand so customers can use it in their own decisions and systems. You don't compete on features. You compete on accuracy, coverage and freshness.

<aside>
ℹ️

Think about which data your solutions are touching and how separate from your current solution they would bring value form the business.

</aside>

**Nielsen**

Nielsen doesn't make a single TV show. It just tells the industry who watched, and billions in advertising are priced on that number.

**PRODUCTIZED SERVICE**

This turns custom expert work, into a repeatable offering with a fixed scope and price, often packaged as software with tools. 

---

**Databricks**

Building data and AI pipelines used to mean hiring a team of specialists for every project. Databricks packaged that expertise into a ready-to-use platform and Solution Accelerators so customers get expert-level results without hiring the experts.

### New Nature of AI Products

> From deterministic to probabilistic.. here are the new ways we build and use AI Products as Product Managers.
> 

**LOOPS & LAYERS**

Projects run in cycles. Self-healing products run in loops and layers, where each layer’s output is the next one’s input:

1. **Sense** - what users do, who they are, what's changing in the macro space.
2. **Synthesise** - signals from layer one are cross-referenced against business goals and a fresh tech-business hypothesis is printed out.
3. **Ship** - the smallest slice that moves the KPI is automatically getting built by agents with HITL.
4. **Validate** - synthetic audience, previews, release rings make sure our rollout plan scales as we hit specific KPIs.

<aside>
ℹ️

With each loop our product becomes smarter, iterations get cheaper and faster as we move towards a [self-healing product](https://dusankolenovic.github.io/self-healing/).

</aside>

---

**SPEED:** 

No one is holding their breath for large release cycles, as they get replaced by a shorter cadence of slices deployment.

Ship → Measure → Repeat

You will know you’re at the right pace when your release notes start looking like a chat tread.

<aside>
ℹ️

**Spotify**

Spotify now ships about 4,500 production deployments a day. Getting from idea to production used to take weeks or months and now takes about an hour. 

</aside>

---

**BUILDING AHEAD OF TIME IS NORMAL**

Classical roadmaps commit only to what today's technology can reliably deliver. AI Products **deliberately** ship a capability before the model is fully ready to support it. For the first time in history you are literally building for tomorrow.

<aside>
ℹ️

**Anthropic** built the harness for its code review feature before the model was good enough. The feature shipped as soon as model accuracy crossed the trust threshold the team had set. The team keeps a shelf of such prototypes and re-tests them with every model release.

</aside>

---

**COMPOUNDING PRODUCTS**

Traditional products treated each feature as a bespoke output that ships once and gets used many times. 

With AI principles, the compounding value of solutions gets unleashed, so AI way makes your solutions smarter (and cheaper) over time and extends their application across multiple different cases (personas, industries etc) in a more seamless manner.

---

**DYNAMIC PRODUCTS**

Traditional tools are built once and stay roughly as capable as the day they shipped. An AI solution decays without active ownership and maintenance of its guardrails and eval context. 

---

**CLEANING IS PART OF BUILDING**

Every model release is a signal to remove something. 

Prompts, guardrails and workarounds built for older models now add unnecessary complexity and should be revisited.

<aside>
ℹ️

On every release, go through your harness setup and ask what you can take out. Anthropic cut 20% of its system prompt in one model upgrade.⁠

</aside>

**TEAMS NOW OWN PROBLEMS**

The classical team is organized by function - product, design, engineering, quality. 

The AI-native team reorganizes around the problem it needs to solve.

<aside>
ℹ️

**DoorDash**

DoorDash found that small teams of 3–5 people, each owning a domain end-to-end, move 3–5x faster with AI.

**PRODUCT CONSISTENCY WILL GET JEOPARDIZED**

Although each slice is deployed successfully and with a purpose, fast deployment in slices will leave your customer experience fragmented and adding friction  breaking the user's mental model. Continuous usability testing and great partnership with the QA crew is the of the whole journey keeps it feeling like one product.⁠

**YOU HAVE COMPLETELY NEW ADOPTION FRICTIONS FOR YOUR SOLUTIONS**

Identity threat

Expert users resist AI tools that imply "the machine knows better." Design AI in your solutions to support their judgment, not replace it. 

---

Automation bias

Users who trust the AI too much stop checking it. Be transparent with confidence levels and sources, and send uncertain cases to a human.

---

Accountability gaps

AI solutions are still tools. If no one knows who owns an AI-assisted decision, it’s adoption might drop. Put a named person behind every decision.

---

The execution part of your job is getting automated: backlog grooming, PRD drafting, ticket writing, status updates. 

What stays is judgment, taste and vision: choosing the right problems to solve, defining what "good" looks like, looking ahead on the business and model capability and designing dozens the guardrails and eval sets.

### Judgment. Taste. Vision - Uniquely Human.

> Models are always trained on what was already done. They have no access to what is felt or shared experience behind genuine connection as these topics don’t have a ground truth for them to work from. This is why when every operational aspect of your work as a Product Manager gets automated: Your Judgement, Vision and Taste are left. This is your authentic human perspective on work.
> 

**HOW DO I KNOW WHAT TO HAND OVER TO AI?**

There are three flavours to choose from and here is the cheat sheet below:

**AUTOMATION**

AI takes over the entire process with 0 supervision.

The process is repetitive, has clear steps you can write in advance, it’s tedious, not creative and always has the same goal. 

Every run takes the same path of steps and "good" output can simply be defined and clearly measured. 

Errors are cheap and easy to spot.

<aside>
ℹ️

*examples:*

- Pulling name, date and total from an invoice.
- Turning a list of specs into a product description.
- Localising documentation while keeping its tone.
</aside>

**AUGMENTATION**

Human and AI work together. AI handles the volume while human handles the judgment. 

A good workflow candidate for Augmentation: 

Has high volume and is pattern-rich, but the stakes are high, so accountability stays with the expert. This expert verifies AI’s output on quality, viability and compliance. 

"Good" is judged by eye, not by a test. There is no objective pass or fail so AI acts as a sparring partner surfacing outputs while the human sets the direction for what’s “good”.

<aside>
ℹ️

*examples:*

- AI drafts the requirements and design spec from the PM's intent, applying brand, security and UX rules as it writes. The product owner reviews the spec instead of writing it, as augmentation moves the human from creator to curator.
- AI builds three throwaway prototypes of a new screen, so the team clicks through them and picks a direction by eye before any real code is written.
</aside>

**AGENTIC SOLUTIONS**

AI acts on its own toward a goal and decides alone on the next step at runtime. It needs boundaries but it doesn’t follow a step-by-step script because the right sequence of steps could change from run to run. A process is a good candidate for an agentic solution when:

The goal is clear, but the path isn't. The work has to be broken into subtasks, progress evaluated along the way, not at the end so the plan gets adjusted along the way. 

<aside>
ℹ️

A tell tale sign you need this workflow is when you notice your automated workflow needed many exceptions

</aside>

It spans many sources across multiple data formats and needs to recall its memory to do its work. 

<aside>
ℹ️

Monitoring an industry news feed and alerting legal only when a regulatory change affects [current products](https://dusan-kolenovic.github.io/clean-slate-watch/). 

Extracting liability clauses from 50 contracts and flagging the ones that deviate from the standard.

</aside>

<aside>
ℹ️

Of course this is a spectrum = The same workflow moves from augmentation toward automation as trust and specification improve. Also with each model release the line moves between these flows so do revisit your decisions here. 

</aside>

### What to build ?

A central question for every Product Manager. 

> **WHAT you decide to build is as equally important as - HOW you decide. Because the quality of an outcome is reflected in the quality of the process behind it.**
> 

The critical datasource to help you navigate this complex question is - Production reality. 

**Production reality exists long before your solution does.** It represents how exactly is the work done today: the real people, the real workflows, the real data, the tools they already juggle and the workarounds they built to get through the day. Your task as a Product Manager is to become familiar with it on two levels. Because your product will not land in empty space. It lands inside a rich context of actual workflows and psychological expectations and motivations of your stakeholders. Understanding both marks the difference between the audience accepting or rejecting your solution.

**This is why:** 

- **adoption is your single most tactical KPI**
- **business goal is your single  strategic KPI**

**ROADMAP**

<aside>
ℹ️

A Roadmap is a hypothesised sequence of functionalities/solutions at a given moment, which is estimated to bring the biggest ROI in shortest amount of time.

</aside>

By its nature it is a snapshot in time, it is a dynamic artefact and you own it. It changes as you develop and discover.

There's tech roadmap and product roadmap, no person X and person Y roadmap (if they work in the same team), not tech-debt and regular development roadmap. There is one product unified roadmap.

It can carry infrastructure, features, design and process work. Not all of it touches users, but all of it creates value for the product.

When Roadmap milestones try to focus on outcomes.

**NEW SHAPES**

Depending who you’re speaking with, there are more than one ways represent a roadmap. 

**1. Feature roadmap** - what gets built and roughly when. Still the right shape for regulatory or compliance deadlines, mature and predictable solutions and low-risk work on known tech.

**2. Outcome roadmap** - instead of a list of features, each team gets a business objective and decides how to reach it. This is much more adjusted to the AI space.

<aside>
ℹ️

</aside>

**3. Strategic intent** - for leadership and non-technical stakeholders. No features and no dates: where we want to be, why it matters and which constraints guide us. In this format, the team keeps the freedom to choose the path.

<aside>
ℹ️

"By Q4, cut median support ticket resolution time by 50% for mid-market customers, without adding headcount." Key bets: smarter ticket routing, auto-replies for simple cases, predictive escalation. Constraints: GDPR/SOC2 and CSAT stays at 85% or higher. If a bet doesn't work, the team drops it without asking for new approval.

### Roll-out & GTM

<aside>
ℹ️

Over time the goal is for your product to become it’s own revenue driver, thus decreasing the pressure on paid marketing. This is the AI Product-led growth, conceptualised as a [self-healing product](https://dusankolenovic.github.io/self-healing/). Until this is achieved the following aspects remain critical and in your control.

</aside>

**ROLL-OUT**

As AI models get updated and customers bring inputs you never tested on - **your product can change after launch without anyone touching a line of code**.

---

A rollout is a continuous process of releasing your product to your audience in which **each stage is unlocked via measured criteria.** 

Here is a standard checklist for releasing AI solutions.

---

**1. WRITTEN CONDITIONS FOR ROLL-OUT**

Each functionality you deploy is competing for its spot on production. 

<aside>
ℹ️

 Have in written what it needs to achieve in each deployment phase to progress to the next one.

</aside>

*Rollout example:*

- **Phase 0 - shadow mode:** the AI runs on real production cases next to the current process, and no end-user sees its output.
    
    *Next phase is unlocked when the AI matches the current process on 99% of cases.*
    
- **Phase 1 - friendly customers:** 3–5 existing end users who agree to try a beta version and give feedback in a structured manner you setup.
    
    *Next phase is unlocked when these users naturally switch over to the new solution in majority of cases without being asked and no critical issue is open.*
    
- **Phase 2 - one segment:** 5% of accounts in one group of similar customers (segment by usage type, pricing tier, geography etc).
    
    *Next phase is unlocked when task success is at least 5% higher than in the group without your solution, and the cost per decision of your AI is within the budget.*
    
- **Phase 3 - all segments:** 25% of accounts across every user group
    
    *Next phase is unlocked when the same lift holds in each group individually, not only on average.*
    
- **Phase 4 - everyone:** all accounts.
    
    *The old path is switched off and everyone uses the new solution.*
    

If a solution misses its condition for a specific phase and in its dedicated time, you can stop the progression to following phase and iterate or roll back with a feature flag (depending on the severity of the miss).

---

These conditions are to be shared with stakeholders before the rollout so they are clear on any promotion or a rollback activity.

---

<aside>
ℹ️

**Stripe** deploys continuously and runs 5.5 nines on its core APIs. Each change goes to a small number of machines first, then 1% of traffic, then a progressive rollout, so no single release carries the big risk.

</aside>

---

**2. MAP THE MENTAL MODELS WAITING IN PRODUCTION**

Your product lands on expectations customers already hold and mental frameworks developed from previous solutions.

Before launch, list the ones it will meet on day one and plan the development and rollout around them as a way to manage expectations.

<aside>
ℹ️

**Anthropic** reports first-attempt success of about one third for its coding agent, so it sets the expectation up front: try one-shot, then switch to collaborating.

</aside>

<aside>
ℹ️

**Notion** saw 18,000 agents created in the three weeks after launching External Agents, with 90% of usage running on automated triggers rather than chat. Customers used agents as standing processes, not as chatbots. Usage data, not the launch plan, showed how the product was actually adopted.

</aside>

---

**3. LET THE COST OF AN ERROR SET THE PACE**

The actual dynamic and guardrails of solution’s progression through roll-out phases, depends on two things:

- how expensive is an error
- how quickly you would spot it

If it’s cheap and visible (the solution writes a draft for the user to edit before sending) - you can take bigger steps in your rollout plan.

*For example 5% → 50% of accounts, about a week per phase.*

---

If it’s costly or hidden or more complex start in parallel with the existing process, then go 1% → 5% → 20%, about a month per phase, with human approval on every action.

---

Do mind, when running the new and old process in parallel: The old process still makes mistakes too, so compare them against its measured error rate as well, not just against the correct rate.

**4. DESIGN ADOPTION, THEN MEASURE IT PER SEGMENT**

Adoption is designed.

If possible try following these rules to convey a frictionless experience of your AI solution.

- Put the entry point inside the workflow segment where the work actually happens, not in a separate AI tab. You want to minimize product surfaces and cognitive load.
- Usage unlocks usage. Figure out a way to track actions as kinetic energy which spreads and drives more usage. Statistics is your friend here ([key concepts](https://app.notion.com/p/Key-analytical-concepts-1f6bcb328fd78095821fef492139a198?pvs=21)) in terms of understanding how user actions are connected.

<aside>
ℹ️

Facebook famously calculated that if a newly registered user added **7 friends within their first 10 days**, they reached the "Aha!moment." Beyond that threshold, their likelihood of becoming a active, long-term user **skyrocketed**. If they failed to reach that milestone, they **almost always** churned.

</aside>

**5. MEASURE THE OUTCOME YOUR CUSTOMER BOUGHT**

Logins show the product is open, not that it works. Pick the one number that proves the customer got the result (a resolved ticket, a completed task) and report it next to usage.

<aside>
ℹ️

**Intercom** prices its Fin agent at $0.99 per confirmed resolution, so the outcome metric is also used as a clear way to invoice customers and show value

**PRICE IS AN EXCHANGE RATE**

Pricing is the exchange rate between the value your customer receives/percieves and what they pay for it. This is the reason why you should get close to understanding **how your end users interpret value from your product.**

The gap between what customers would pay and what you charge **is your revenue left on the table**.

<aside>
ℹ️

Patrick Campbell's (ProfitWell) pricing data shows most companies underprice. "Finger in the air" pricing almost always means you're underselling.

</aside>

---

**FIND YOUR VALUE METRIC**

A value metric is the unit you charge for: seats, messages, rows synced, resolved tickets, API calls. A good value metric has three attributes:

- It is easy to understand
    - A customer should understand what they'll pay without a calculator.
- Grows as your customer succeeds
- Feels fair to your customers

There are four common ways to build a pricing strategy around your value metric:

- **Feature tiers** - pay more for advanced features (Adobe: Photoshop only vs. the full suite)
- **Light features + value metric** - few differences between plans, the price follows usage (Slack per user)
    - similar ⬆️ ⬇️
- **Hybrid** - feature tiers plus a value metric (Salesforce plans priced per user)
- **Pure value metric** - the price follows usage only (Twilio per message or API call)

**RESEARCH, DON'T GUESS**

Don't assume what customers value and how much they value the thing. There are simple willingness to pay excersizes you can use to learn from your customers.

- *learn more: which method when*
    - **Van Westendorp** - early on, when you need a broad price range.
    - **Gabor-Granger** - once the offer is clear. It asks about purchase intent at set price points to find the price that realistically brings the most revenue.
    - **Conjoint** **analyses**- The most holistic & precise, but also the most complex of the three

**PRICING IN TIMES OF AI**

The fundamental change came in when outside of classic infra cost, new AI products produce increased inference cost just by being used. 

This means that the old freemium model in which users had unlimited access to the product and its functionalities has to change. 

The freemium today has to show enough "magic" for users to hit the aha moment, so the free tier can end up good enough to replace the paid one.

When setting this up, the question on Product Manager’s table is: “How to decide what to gate behind a paywall”” as freemium works as great acquisition engine.

---

Google AI started with one premium tier built around its smartest model. It didn't work: the free tier already felt "smarter than I am" to most users, so few upgraded, while the power users who did pay burned so much compute that the economics were "terrifying". 

Here is what they did:

<aside>
ℹ️

Google AI's product lead puts three things behind the paywall instead:

- **Usage intensity** - this means tiers by volume. Midjourney gives every plan the same image quality: you pay for fast GPU hours, and once they run out, your images wait in a slower queue.
- **Outcomes** - free gives you the answer, paid does the work. Chrome auto browse, where the agent clicks through websites and finishes the task for you, is only in Google's higher tiers and is sold on the hours of manual work it saves.
- **The heaviest modalities** - text stays free, video, 3D and simulation are paid. Genie 3, Google's real-time interactive world model, is top tier only. Serving it to hundreds of millions of free users wasn't just bad business, there weren't enough chips to do it.
</aside>

**MONETIZATION**

You have a new cost line item in your product work.

It's the price of every decision your AI makes for your customer. Each one is paid in model calls, tokens, retrieval and tool runs, so it is important to track it per decision, not only per customer.

<aside>
ℹ️

**Dropbox** saw its spend on AI agents rise about 28x in a year. Instead of cutting it, the team started tracking cost per pull request and per Jira ticket, to see whether the money was going to the right problems.

</aside>

---

Pricing sets the number. Monetization is the mechanics behind it: how the bill is built, how it behaves month to month and whether each customer still earns you money.

---

**COST TO SERVE SETS THE FLOOR**

Your cost per decision sets the floor of your price. The dynamic nature of pricing is enhanced even further by the fact that, **that floor moves with every model you choose and every new model release.**

<aside>
ℹ️

On the Databricks pricing calculator the same workload costs $3,823 a month on GPT-5 and $152 on GPT-5 Nano. Model choice is now a monetization decision.

</aside>

---

**PREDICTABILITY IS A FEATURE**

Metered pricing is harder to predict than a flat fee. When a bill surprises, customers don't just question the price, they stop trusting the product. Bill shock is a product problem, not a billing one.

<aside>
ℹ️

**Fivetran** charges per monthly active row (MAR). Over 2025–26 it moved volume discounts from the account to each connection and started charging for deleted and history rows. A backfill or schema change could add millions of billable rows overnight, and customers who thought they had 1M active rows had 4M. Many bills rose 40–70%, some 2–4x, and competitors built their pitch around it.

</aside>

The fix is a structure your customer can budget for:

- a committed base they agree to upfront
- overage on top, priced in advance
- real-time usage visibility, so alerts arrive before the spend and not with the invoice

<aside>
ℹ️

**Lebara** eSIM customers got bill shock coming back from holidays. Instead of handling it in support, the team built proactive notifications: a usage alert at 80% and a one-tap top-up. Predictability became a feature, not a support process.

</aside>

Customers stay on usage-based plans they trust.

---

**AI KEEPS THE BILL UNDER CONTROL**

The same AI that adds a cost line also helps you manage the bill:

- forecast usage per cohort and flag anomalies before the invoice goes out
- answer "why did my bill change?" with the actual root cause
- replay a planned price change on real customer bills before you ship it, so you see who gets hit and by how much

In terms of Product KPIs for agentic solutions - they differ from classic ones and are grouped below:

- **Quality & Trust**
    - Eval coverage - % of real production scenarios represented in your eval/golden set
    - Eval pass rate as a merge/release gate
    - Hallucination ratio
    - Regeneration rate - how often a user hits "retry/regenerate"
    - Guardrail trigger / policy-violation hit rate by the autonomous solution
    - Trace coverage - % of decisions with a complete, auditable trace/log
    - Re-engagement after error rate
- **Autonomy & Reliability**
    - Autonomy rate based on task complexity
        - how many of core complex tasks are confidently covered by AI vs surface level activities
    - Types of tasks automated (based on complexity)
    - Ratio of AI decision override by human
    - Model drift frequency
- **Value & Adoption**
    - Depth / organic (unprompted) adoption
        - Depth-of-use progression
    - Goal completion rate
    - Time-to-Data / Time-to-Value
    - Flywheel coverage - ratio of quality outputs fed back to the system
    - Synthetic-audience accumulation rate
- **Unit Economics**
    - Inference cost-per-decision, weighed against the value each call creates
    - Cache hit rate
